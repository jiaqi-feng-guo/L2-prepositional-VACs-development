# Error taxonomy

Errors are coded with a five-category taxonomy. Three categories are surface-level, adapted from the Surface Strategy Taxonomy (Dulay, Burt, & Krashen, 1982). Two are construction-specific and were developed for this study. The `Error-Type-New` column carries the five-category label used in all statistics; `Error-Type-Old` preserves a finer split within Collocation.

## Surface-level categories

**Omission** — the absence of a required element, typically the verb or copula the construction needs to be grammatically complete.

**Addition** — the presence of an element the construction does not require, such as a superfluous preposition or a redundant modifier that must be deleted to repair the string.

**Misordering** — positional errors, such as swapping the two argument roles, or an adverb or modifier placed in the wrong slot.

## Construction-specific categories

**Collocation** — the pairing of verb, preposition, and argument is grammatically possible but is not the pairing that conventional usage favours. The string violates no syntactic rule, yet native speakers systematically prefer a different verb, preposition, or combination. The intended function is one that *duì* expresses; what is mispaired is the verb. Two forms are recorded separately and combined for analysis:

- `Collocation_Prep` — the verb selects a different preposition but *duì* is used instead. Example: 对他送礼物 (*duì tā sòng lǐwù*, 'give him a gift'), where 送 (*sòng*, 'give') takes 给 (*gěi*) rather than 对, giving 给他送礼物 (*gěi tā sòng lǐwù*).
- `Collocation_VA` — the verb takes its object directly and needs no preposition, so *duì* is superfluous. Example: 对我们问 (*duì wǒmen wèn*, intended 'ask us'), where Chinese says 问我们 (*wèn wǒmen*, 'ask us').

**Non-*duì*-construction** — *duì* is used for a meaning that requires a different construction altogether. Here the intended function is not a *duì* function at all, which is what distinguishes this category from Collocation. Example: a learner writes 对我很熟悉 (*duì wǒ hěn shúxī*), intending 'familiar to me', but in the bare *duì* frame this reads as the subject knowing the speaker well; the intended perspective meaning requires 对我来说很熟悉 (*duì wǒ lái shuō hěn shúxī*, 'to me, it is very familiar'). For these errors the intended target is recorded in the `Intended Function` column.
